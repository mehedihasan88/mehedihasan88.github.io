About

I am Mehedi Hasan, a Software Engineer at Samsung Research Bangladesh, where I work on WearOS-based Samsung Watch applications. As part of the Wearable Solution Team, I integrate AI and automation into workflows to improve productivity and contribute to the development of wearable device specific features.

I completed my BSc and MSc in Computer Science & Engineering from Jahangirnagar University, where I developed strong skills in algorithm design and competitive programming through extensive problem-solving. My work has been recognized with excellence awards at Samsung for development and research contributions, along with a Best Paper Award at the Bangla Language Processing (BLP) Workshop (IJCNLP-AACL 2025).

My research interests include Agentic AI, Multimodal AI, and Trustworthy AI, with a focus on improving the reasoning, reliability, and explainability of intelligent systems for real-world applications.

Publications

Segmentation Strategies for Long-Code Authorship Detection: Balancing Context Coverage and Computational Efficiency

Mehedi Hasan, Mahbub Islam Mahim, Jugal Krishna Das (2026) · Submitted

Compared truncation, multi-view cropping, and sliding-window segmentation for long-code authorship detection under transformer context limits. Sliding-window achieved the best performance; multi-view reached near-parity with far fewer computations—highlighting context coverage as the main driver of accuracy.

submitted

Divide and Conquer: A Two-Stage Label-Wise Cascaded Framework for Bangla Hate Speech Detection

Mehedi Hasan, Mahbub Islam Mahim (2025) · Best Paper, BLP Workshop 2025 · Presented at AACL-IJCNLP 2025

Cascaded pipeline combining binary and multiclass models with k-fold cross-validation and intra-label ensembling; achieved micro-F1 0.7392—top result in the BLP Workshop 2025 shared task.

Layerwise-Probes-Ablations-in-BERT

Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers · BLP Workshop 2025 shared task

Analyzed BERT’s 12-layer encoder using linear probing and layer ablation to distinguish layer-wise responsibilities. Demonstrated that mid-layers (5–6) yield the most separable features, while upper layers (10–11) drive final predictions in parts-of speech tagging and semantic similarity task
